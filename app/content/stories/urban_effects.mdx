  # Introduction

  Driven by decades of sustained population growth, metropolitan areas across the United States have undergone extensive infrastructural development across residential, commercial, and industrial sectors. The resulting proliferation of artificial surfaces fundamentally modifies the landscape, yielding profound consequences for local meteorological and Earth systems.

  The Miami metropolitan region (MMR) effectively illustrates this phenomenon. Having recorded the fourth-largest population increase among U.S. metropolitan areas between 2023 and 2024 (per the U.S. Census Bureau), the MMR faces compounded environmental risks. Because the region is situated largely at sea level, urbanization threatens to exacerbate its inherent susceptibility to hurricanes and flooding.
